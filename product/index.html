Everything it does, on your computer.

Aldergrove is built for the small jobs that fill a day: reminders, follow-ups, mail and quick answers. It runs on your Mac, so it costs nothing to keep working and your data stays put. Here is what it can do today. Features marked Beta are built and tested in code, and still being tried with real accounts.

01 Everyday jobs Beta

Small jobs, all day. Free, and private.

Reminders, schedules, follow-ups, mail and calendar. Aldergrove runs on your Mac, so a job that checks in every hour costs nothing, and your mail never passes through us. Before anything is sent, posted, booked or deleted, one card shows who it goes to, the exact content, and whether it can be undone.

04 Set up for you

One button. The right model for your Mac.

Aldergrove reads how much memory your computer has, picks the version of Linden 1 that fits with room left for your apps, and downloads it. It runs in Aldergrove's own engine, so there's nothing else to install.

Its own engine

Models run in Aldergrove's built-in runner, about twice as fast as Ollama on a Mac. Ollama isn't needed.

Your work goes first

A queue in front of the model puts your chats ahead of titles, notes and background jobs.

Honest waiting

While the model gets ready, the chat says what it's doing: starting, loading, or reading with a measured percent.

Cloud is optional

Bring your own keys if you want a cloud model. Each task shows what it cost; local models are free.

05 Code that's checked

Done means the tests pass.

06 It looks at its work

It opens the page it built, and measures it.

After a frontend change, Aldergrove renders the page at desktop and phone width, takes screenshots and checks them. Every problem is boxed where it is, then sent back to be fixed.

Text too faint: 2.1:1 Wider than the screen

Ten checks

Sideways scrolling, faint text, cut-off or overlapping text, broken images, a phone menu that won't open, dead links and more.

Design from a reference

Point it at a site you like. It studies the fonts, sizes, colours and widths, then compares.

Photo check

Checks that pictures show what the page says they do, and that every image has a file.
